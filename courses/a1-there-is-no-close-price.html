Learn Crypto with Phoebe · Leader session 1 of 6

There is no close price

A conventional market feed hands a data team one number a day without being asked: the close. It exists because the market stops, settles and reopens, so the last print of the session is an official event with a timestamp everybody shares. A crypto market tape never stops. Nothing settles, no bell rings, and the last print before midnight is not more official than the last print before lunch. Tonight is what that does to the most ordinary sentence in a report, "yesterday's return", measured on a constructed five-day tape where the same prints give four different answers depending on when you decided the day ends. Nothing here is investment advice, and nothing here is a price of anything real.

Part 0

Harrowgate, on the morning the report asks for yesterday's return

Harrowgate is a constructed data team at a fund-administration firm. It has been asked to publish, every morning, a reference level for an abstract asset and a one-line daily return, the way it already does for a dozen conventional instruments. The team is competent and the pipeline exists. The first morning on the crypto feed, two analysts compute yesterday's return from the same prints and get different signs. Neither has made an arithmetic error. Tonight is about why, and what the honest sentence in the report looks like.

LiveWhat a conventional feed quietly provides4 min▶

Ask a data leader what their market-data pipeline depends on and you get vendors, entitlements and a warehouse. The things it actually leans on are quieter: a session that opens and closes, so there is one official last price a day; one consolidated record of every trade, so "the price" means something; volume counted by an operator with an auditor behind it; a currency that is stable enough to be the ruler rather than the thing being measured; and counterparties who are, at some level, identified.

None of those is a technology choice. They are properties of the arrangement, and they are so reliable on a conventional feed that the whole reporting craft is built on top of them without a mention. A crypto market tape removes all five at once. That is why the experience is disorienting rather than merely unfamiliar: the arithmetic is the same, the assumptions under the arithmetic are gone.

Real world

Harrowgate's first week went badly for a reason nobody predicted. Nobody lacked skill. Every recovery move the team reached for assumed at least one of the five: they looked for the official close, they looked for the consolidated tape, they ranked venues by the volume each venue reported. Each of those is the right instinct somewhere else. Harrowgate is constructed; the mistakes are the ordinary ones.

Self-studyThe words that keep coming back4 min▶
  • Tape. The sequence of executed trades a venue publishes: a timestamp, a side, a size and a price per row. The only thing that happened. Practitioner session 1.
  • Print. One row of the tape. "The last print before the boundary" is what a close is made of.
  • Boundary. The moment you decide a day ends. On a conventional feed the market decides; here you do. Tonight.
  • Candle. Open, high, low, close and volume over a chosen interval. A derived object, never an observation. Practitioner session 2.
  • Venue. One exchange, with its own tape, its own book and its own reported volume. Session 3 is about having many of them.
  • Reported volume. The number a venue publishes about itself. Session 2 is about why that is a claim rather than a measurement.
  • Unit of account. The thing prices are quoted in. On this feed it is often itself a traded asset, and session 4 is about what happens when it moves.
  • Reference level. The number an index publishes after reconciling venues. Session 3 and practitioner session 3.
Part 1 · covers why there is no close

Nothing closes, so the close is a choice

15 min live

A close price is not a property of prices. It is a property of a market that stops. The conventional session has an open, a close, and a settlement step that turns the last print into an official number with a shared timestamp. Take the stopping away and every print is just a print. Somebody has to draw a line through the tape and call the print before it "the close", and whoever draws the line is choosing the number.

LiveThe settlement day is a service, not a fact of nature5 min▶

On a conventional feed the close arrives as if it were an observation. It is not. It is the output of an operator stopping a market, running a closing procedure, and publishing a number under a shared timestamp that every downstream consumer agrees to use. The data team never has to decide anything, which is exactly why it never notices that a decision was made.

A crypto venue runs continuously. There is no closing procedure because there is no closing. The tape at 23:59:59 is the same kind of thing as the tape at 11:00:00: a sequence of prints. If a data team wants "the close", it has to pick a boundary, take the last print before it, and call that the close. Three teams picking three boundaries get three closes and all of them are correct. What is wrong is any report that prints one of them without saying which.

The boundary is not the only thing missing. The overnight gap on a conventional feed is a stretch with no prints at all, so "yesterday's close to today's open" is a clean comparison. Here the stretch is full of prints, so a return computed between any two boundaries is also a return over a period that contains a whole night of trading nobody in the room was watching.

LiveFour conventions, four returns, two signs5 min▶

The constructed tape in this page runs five days with 3,000 prints a day. Under a midnight boundary the first full-day return is +0.052 percent. Move the boundary to 08:00 and the same day reads -0.020 percent. At 16:00 it reads -0.061 percent, and at 21:00 it reads +0.054 percent. Nothing about the prints changed. Only the line somebody drew through them.

The sizes are small, which is the point rather than a weakness. On a tape with no trend, the entire day-over-day return is made of where the boundary happens to fall between prints. A reader who sees "-0.061 percent" in a report and "+0.052 percent" in another will assume one source is wrong. Both are right, and both are unfinished until the boundary is written beside them.

One more detail worth noticing in the widget below. The midnight convention prints four returns on a five-day tape and the others print five, because the first midnight boundary sits at the very start of the tape with no print before it. That is not a bug in the widget. A boundary with nothing before it has no close, and a feed that starts at midnight has one fewer day than it looks like.

Self-studyWhere serious people disagree4 min▶

One camp holds that the only honest daily number on a continuous market is a fixing: a reference level published at a stated time of day by a stated method, the way a public index rulebook does it, with a calculation window and partitions rather than a single last print. On this view "the close" should never appear in a report at all; the fixing time and the method appear instead, and the daily return is a return between fixings.

The other camp holds that a fixing is just a boundary with a committee behind it, and that the right move is to stop pretending there is a day. Report rolling 24-hour windows, or report the full distribution of prints in the period, and let the reader see that "yesterday" is a shape rather than a point. On this view the fixing is a comfort for people who want the old sentence back.

Both camps agree on the thing that matters tonight: the number does not exist until the convention does, and the convention has to travel with the number. Which camp the person across the table belongs to tells you how they will react when two reports disagree.

Part 2 · covers Harrowgate

Harrowgate's report, and the sentence it has to put in it

10 min live

Harrowgate's mechanism is five steps long and there is nothing exotic in any of them. A tape arrives. Somebody picks a boundary. The last print before it becomes the close. The close is compared with the previous close. A number goes in the report. Each step is ordinary. One of them is a choice that a conventional feed makes for you, and it is the step the report never mentions.

LiveWhy the convention column is the honest one3 min▶

Change the boundary and watch the first full-day return on Tape A move from +0.052 to -0.020 to -0.061 to +0.054 percent. Then look at what did not move: the final close is 100.306 under every convention, because the tape ends at the same print whichever line you drew. That is the giveaway. The close is only stable where the tape happens to stop, and a real tape never stops.

A data team's reflex at this point is to go and find the right boundary. There is no right boundary. There is a stated one, and the professional norm on any continuous market is that the statement sits beside the number, in the same cell or the same sentence, so that two reports can be compared without a phone call. Practitioner session 2 builds the candles that make the choice explicit; practitioner session 3 shows what the public index rulebooks do instead of a last print.

LiveThe other four things the conventional feed quietly provided3 min▶

The close is the first thing Harrowgate noticed missing because it is the first thing a daily report needs. It is not the biggest. There is no consolidated tape, so "the price" is one venue's last print at one moment and four venues give four defensible answers at once (session 3). Every venue reports its own volume with no auditor and a league table to climb, so the most-quoted number in the market is a claim rather than a measurement (session 2). The unit everything is quoted in is itself a traded thing, so a chart can move because the ruler moved (session 4). And nobody on the tape is identified, so a question like "how many participants" has no column that answers it.

All five change at the same time, which is why none of them can be handled on its own. Every workaround for one of them quietly assumes another still holds. Session 5 takes the five apart as mechanisms; session 6 says what a data team already knows that is worth more here than it was before.

Self-studyNot investment advice, and what that means for a data team4 min▶

Nothing in these sessions is advice about buying, selling or holding anything, and no page quotes a real price, a real venue or a real asset. That is a content rule, and it is also the professional stance of a data team on this feed. Harrowgate's job is not to have a view on the asset. It is to publish a number whose provenance a stranger can check: which venues, which boundary, which method, which exclusions.

The discipline pays off in an unexpected place. A team that writes the convention beside every number is a team whose numbers can be reproduced by someone who disagrees with them, which is the only kind of number worth defending in a dispute. On a conventional feed the operator's settlement procedure provides that reproducibility for free. Here the team has to provide it itself, every morning, in the report.

Part 3 · build-along

Find the boundaries hiding in your own reports

17 min hands on

No code. Six steps on a number your own team publishes today, said out loud in the room. The aim is to notice how many conventions a conventional feed has been choosing for you, because those are the ones that will bite first on a tape that never stops.

Name the five from memory

Without looking up: a session that closes, one consolidated tape, audited volume, a stable unit of account, identified participants. Say what each one gives a daily report. The one most people cannot state is the fourth, which is also the one with no engineering fix.

Pick a daily number you publish

Something real, with a return or a change that goes to someone senior. Write down the boundary it uses. If the answer is "the close", write down who decided when the close is, and whether anyone on your team could state it without looking.

Move the boundary by eight hours in your head

For a continuous source, ask whether the sign of yesterday's number could plausibly flip. On Tape A it did, twice. If you cannot say how large the flip could be, you do not yet know how much of your number is the market and how much is the line.

Find the cell where the convention would go

Look at the report layout. Is there a place for "16:00 boundary, last print, venue A" beside the number? If there is not, the report was designed for a feed that made the choice for you, and the first thing to change is the layout, not the pipeline.

Count the other four

For the same number: is the source consolidated or one venue? Is its volume audited or self-reported? Is the quoting unit stable or traded? Are the participants identified? Mark each as "provided by the feed" or "we would have to provide it". The second column is the size of the job.

Commit to a sentence

Write the finished version of "yesterday's return was X" for a continuous source, with every convention in it, on paper where you can see it. Session 2 adds the word that has to go beside the volume figure, and it is not "audited".

The habit to keep. When someone hands you a daily number taken from a market that never stops, ask which boundary made it a day before you ask anything about the market. The arithmetic is almost never wrong. The boundary is almost never stated, and it can carry the whole sign.

Check yourself

Three questions

Two Harrowgate analysts compute yesterday's return from the same prints and get +0.052 percent and -0.061 percent. Which statement is right?

On a continuous tape the close is the last print before a boundary somebody chose. Two boundaries give two closes and two returns, both correct. Averaging them produces a third number with no convention at all, which is worse than either.

Why does a conventional equity feed have a close price when a crypto venue does not?

The close is a product of stopping. A market that never stops has no last print of the session because there is no session, and no amount of trade volume creates one. The data team has to draw the line itself.

On the same five-day tape the midnight convention prints four daily returns and the 08:00 convention prints five. Why?

A close needs a print before the boundary. A tape that begins at midnight has nothing before its first midnight boundary, so that convention has one fewer close than a boundary that falls eight hours in. The count of days is itself a property of the convention.

Before session 2

Homework

Twenty minutes, on a number your own team reports and on the sentence you just wrote.

Write the five down for one report

For one daily number: the boundary, the source, who counts the volume, what the quoting unit is, and whether participants are identified. One line each. Mark the ones you had never articulated before tonight.

Bring one volume figure you trust

Any volume number your team quotes, with the name of whoever produced it. Session 2 asks one question about it: who would have noticed if it were inflated, and what they stood to gain from not noticing.
