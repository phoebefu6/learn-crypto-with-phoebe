Learn Crypto with Phoebe · Leader session 4 of 6

The unit of account can move

On a conventional feed the currency a price is quoted in is the ruler. Nobody measures the ruler, because it does not move within a day in any way a report would notice. On a crypto venue the thing prices are quoted in is very often itself a traded asset, designed to hold 1.000 against a reference and usually doing so to a thousandth. Usually. Tonight is what happens to a report on the morning it does not: a constructed depeg in this page, a quoted tape that stays calm the whole time, and the same prints restated into the reference unit, where the day looks completely different. Nothing here is investment advice, and nothing here is a real unit, a real asset or a real price.

Part 0

Harrowgate, on the morning the ruler slipped

Harrowgate is a constructed data team at a fund-administration firm. After sessions 1 to 3 it publishes a daily reference level for an abstract asset, with the boundary written beside it, the venues screened and the method named. Every venue it reads quotes the asset in the same quoting unit, a traded thing designed to track a reference unit at 1.000. One morning, from 10:00, the quoting unit slips below its reference and stays there for hours. Harrowgate's dashboards show nothing unusual at all. Tonight is about why the calm tape was telling the truth and still hid the day, and what the report has to carry so that it cannot happen quietly again.

★ What you walk out with tonight. The reason every quoted price is a ratio, drawn so the bottom of the ratio is visible for once. A constructed depeg run in this page at four depths, where the quoted tape never moves more than 0.046 percent in an hour and the restated tape falls 3.96 percent in one hour at a 6 percent depth. The single storage decision that makes a restatement possible later, and the three others that make it honest. And the sentence that goes in the report on the morning the unit leaves its band.

LiveThe morning nothing looked wrong3 min▶

Harrowgate's monitoring watches the quoted tape: the last print per venue, the hourly change, the spread between venues. It is good monitoring, built from the conventional playbook, and on the depeg morning every one of its panels stayed green. The largest hour-over-hour move of the day on the quoted tape was a small one at 04:00, six hours before anything happened.

The first sign came from outside the data team. A colleague who settles in the reference unit asked why the reference level looked six percent richer than the value they could actually realise. Nobody on the team had a column that could answer, because nobody had stored the one number that would: what the quoting unit itself was worth, minute by minute, against the reference.

Real world

The pattern is ordinary on any desk that reports in one unit and settles in another: the monitoring watches the number it is given, and the number it is given is already divided by the thing that moved. A conventional fund that holds assets in a second currency meets the same arithmetic at the month-end translation. The difference here is that the quoting unit is designed never to move, so nobody builds the translation step until the day it does. Harrowgate is constructed; the gap in its warehouse is the common one.

Self-studyThe words that keep coming back4 min▶
  • Quoting unit. What the venue's prices are expressed in. Here, a traded thing designed to track a reference. Tonight.
  • Reference unit. What the report's reader thinks in, and what the quoting unit is designed to hold 1.000 against.
  • Peg. The design target, 1.000 quoting unit per reference unit. A target, not a guarantee.
  • Band. How far from 1.000 the unit normally sits. On the constructed path, a thousandth either way.
  • Depeg. The unit leaving its band. On the tape it is a data event before it is anything else.
  • Quoted tape. The prints as the venue published them, in the quoting unit. Practitioner session 1.
  • Restated tape. The same prints multiplied by the unit's level at each print's own minute, so they read in the reference unit. Practitioner session 8 builds it.
  • Unit path. The unit's own level against the reference, minute by minute. The column most warehouses do not have.
Part 1 · covers every price is a ratio

Every price is a ratio, and here the bottom is traded

12 min live

A quoted price is never a single quantity. It is how many quoting units one unit of the asset fetches: asset over quoting unit. On a conventional feed the bottom of that fraction is the currency itself, and the currency is the ruler, so every move on the screen is a move in the top. On this feed the bottom is a traded thing with a market of its own. When it moves, the fraction can stay perfectly still while what it means changes underneath.

LiveA price has a top and a bottom4 min▶

Say "the asset printed at 100" and you have said something about two things: the asset and the unit the 100 is counted in. On a conventional feed the second one is so stable within a day that the sentence drops it, and the whole reporting craft drops it with the sentence. Returns, ranges, volatility, alerts: all of them are computed on the top of the fraction as if the bottom were a constant.

The bottom is a constant only by arrangement. A conventional currency is the thing the venue settles in, the thing the reader's books are kept in, and the thing every other price on the screen is also quoted in. It is the ruler because everybody agreed to measure with it. Change any of those three facts and the bottom of the fraction becomes a quantity with its own behaviour, which a report has to measure like any other.

LiveWhen the ruler is itself traded4 min▶

On many crypto venues the quoting unit is not the reader's currency. It is a traded thing designed to track a reference unit at 1.000, so that prices on the venue feel as if they were in the reference. Most of the time the design works to about a thousandth, and the difference is invisible in any report. The constructed path in this page does exactly that: 1.000 against the reference, give or take a thousandth, minute after minute.

"Designed to track" is a claim about intent, the same way a venue's reported volume in session 2 is a claim about activity. The unit trades in its own market and can sit below the reference for minutes, hours or longer. While it does, every price quoted in it is a fraction whose bottom has moved. The quoted tape has no way to say so, because the quoted tape is the top of the fraction measured in the bottom. The only place the move shows up is in the unit's own level, and that is a second dataset with its own source, its own grain and its own many prices, exactly as session 3 described for the asset.

Self-studyWhere serious people disagree4 min▶

One camp holds that a report should stay in the quoting unit. That is what traded, it is what the venue published, and restating imports a second source, the unit's level, which has its own venues, its own reconciliation problem and its own disputes. On this view a restated number is a derived number with twice the provenance to defend, and the honest report prints what the tape said and flags the unit separately.

The other camp holds that a report should be in the unit its reader thinks in, which is almost never the quoting unit. A reader who settles in the reference unit and is shown a calm quoted tape on a depeg morning has been shown a true number that answers the wrong question. On this view the restated figure is the headline and the quoted one is the footnote.

Both camps agree on the thing that matters tonight: neither number can be produced later unless both the quoted prints and the unit's own level were stored at the time. Which camp the person across the table belongs to decides which figure goes first in the report. It does not decide what goes in the warehouse.

Part 2 · covers a depeg as a data event

A depeg is a data event before it is anything else

15 min live

The constructed depeg is declared, not discovered, so you can see every moving part. The quoting unit sits at 1.000 against the reference with a jitter of a thousandth. From minute 600, which is 10:00, it falls in a straight line for 90 minutes, holds at the bottom for 120 and recovers over 240, back at 1.000 by 17:30. The asset's tape is an honest constructed one-day tape, untouched. Only the ruler moves.

LiveRead the widget at 6 percent4 min▶

Start on Tape A at a 6 percent depth. The quoting unit bottoms at 0.939016 of the reference. The largest hour-over-hour move on the quoted tape is -0.046 percent, at hour 4, six hours before the depeg begins; it is ordinary trading noise. The largest move on the restated tape is -3.96 percent, at hour 10, the hour the unit is falling. The first print at or after noon restates to 94.025 reference units; the widget shows it beside its quoted price, which barely differs from any other print that day.

Nothing about the asset's prints changed between the two columns. The restated tape is the quoted tape multiplied, print by print, by the unit's level at that print's minute. Everything the restated tape shows that the quoted one does not is the unit.

LiveThe depth barely touches the quoted tape4 min▶

Now move the depth. At 3 percent the unit bottoms at 0.969016 and the restated tape's worst hour is -1.99 percent. At 12 percent it bottoms at 0.879016 and the worst hour is -7.89 percent, both at hour 10. The quoted column reads -0.046 percent at hour 4 on every depth, including zero. The quoted tape never moves more than 0.046 percent in an hour at any depth, because the move is in the unit.

Switch tapes and the quoted figure changes, because each constructed tape has its own ordinary noise. What does not change is the shape of the result: on every tape the quoted figure is the same at every depth, and the restated figure tracks the depth. A monitor built on the quoted tape is not broken on a depeg morning. It is measuring exactly what it was built to measure, in a ruler that is moving with it.

Self-studyEven zero depth is not zero4 min▶

Set the depth to 0 on Tape A. There is no depeg at all, the unit never leaves its thousandth, and still the restated tape's worst hour is -0.155 percent, at hour 21, against -0.046 percent quoted. The unit bottoms at 0.999000. That is the jitter: a thousandth up or down in the unit, minute to minute, is enough to make the restated tape noisier than the quoted one on a perfectly ordinary day.

Two consequences for a leader. First, restating is not free: it adds the unit's own noise to every number, so an alert threshold tuned on the quoted tape will fire more often on the restated one, for reasons that have nothing to do with the asset. Second, "the unit left its band" needs a band wider than the jitter, or the flag in Part 3 fires every hour. Where the band sits is a decision, written down once, not a number to discover. How restatement is built at the minute grain, including the as-of join that matches each print to the unit's level, is practitioner session 8; the stream mechanics behind that join live in learn-streaming-data-with-phoebe.

Part 3 · build-along

What the leader decides

15 min hands on

No code. Six steps on a number your own team reports in a unit it did not choose, said out loud in the room. Four of the steps are decisions only a leader can make, because each one costs storage, a column or a sentence somebody has to defend. The other two make them stick.

Name the reference unit the report uses

Write down the unit the report's reader thinks in and settles in. If the answer is "whatever the venue quotes", the report has no reference unit, and a depeg morning will be reported in the ruler that moved. One unit, named on the report, chosen on purpose.

Store both, the quoted and the restated

Keep the quoted prints exactly as the venue published them, and keep the restated figures beside them. The quoted tape is the observation; the restated one is derived. Throwing away either loses something: the observation cannot be recovered from the derivation if the unit's level is later corrected, and the derivation cannot be rebuilt without the next step.

Add a column for the unit's own level

The unit's level against the reference, at the same grain as the prints, minute by minute on the constructed tape. This is the one storage decision that cannot be made later. A restatement uses each print's own minute; a daily average of the unit would put a 10:00 print and a 16:00 print on the same ruler when they were not.

Write down where the unit's level comes from

The unit has its own venues and its own many prices, so its level is a reconciled number with a method, exactly like the asset's in session 3. Name the source and the method in the same place you name the boundary. A restated figure has two provenances, and the report owes the reader both.

Set the band and the flag

Decide how far from 1.000 the unit may sit before the report says so, wider than its ordinary jitter, which is a thousandth on the constructed path. When the unit leaves the band, the report carries a flag beside the headline number, not a footnote: "quoting unit outside its band from 10:00; figures restated into the reference unit".

Commit to the sentence

Write the finished version for a depeg morning, with every decision in it: reference unit, restated figure, quoted figure, the unit's low and where its level came from. Then check it against your own report layout. If there is no cell for the unit's level, the layout was designed for a feed whose ruler never moved.

Real world

Harrowgate's next report changed four things and no arithmetic on the asset. The headline level was stated in the reference unit, with the quoted figure beside it. A new column carried the quoting unit's own level per minute, sourced and reconciled like any other price. A flag appeared beside the headline whenever the unit left a band the team had written down, set wider than its ordinary jitter. And the morning summary gained one line: the unit's low for the day and the hour it happened. On the constructed depeg at 6 percent that line would have read 0.939016, during the hour the restated tape fell 3.96 percent. Harrowgate is constructed; the four changes are the standard ones for any report kept in a unit that can move.

The habit to keep. When a quoted number looks calm on a day that should not be calm, ask what it is divided by before you ask what it measures. A ruler that moves with the thing it measures shows nothing, truthfully.

Check yourself

Three questions

On the constructed 6 percent depeg the quoted tape's worst hour is -0.046 percent while the restated tape's is -3.96 percent. Why does the quoted tape look calm?

The prints kept coming and nothing was smoothed. A quoted price is asset over quoting unit; when the bottom of the fraction moves, the fraction can stay still while what it means changes. The -0.046 percent at hour 4 is ordinary noise from before the depeg began, and it is the same at every depth.

A year later an auditor asks Harrowgate to restate the depeg morning into the reference unit. What must have been stored at the time?

Restating multiplies each print by the unit's level at that print's own minute. A daily average puts a 10:00 print and a 16:00 print on the same ruler when they were not, and restated figures alone cannot be rechecked if the unit's level is ever corrected. The source of the level matters because the unit has its own many prices.

Which number goes in Harrowgate's report on the depeg morning?

The reader thinks and settles in the reference unit, so the headline is in that unit and names it. The quoted figure stays beside it because it is the observation, and the flag tells the reader why the two differ today. An average of two numbers in two different units is in no unit at all.

Before session 5

Homework

Twenty minutes, on a number your own team reports and on the sentence you wrote.

Find the bottom of one fraction

Pick one number your team reports and write down what it is divided by: the currency, a unit, a base period, a headcount. Then ask whether that bottom is stored anywhere with a timestamp. If it is not, you could not restate the number if the bottom ever moved.
