Reconciling venues into an index

There is no consolidated tape, so a reference level is something you compute, not something you look up. Tonight is the computation, done properly: what a venue quote actually is, four estimators written as code you can read line by line, the cumulative-volume search that sits inside a weighted median, two exclusion rules and the venue each one can and cannot see, and the partitioned, time-weighted fixing that one public index rulebook uses instead of anybody's last print. Then the part most teams skip: a provenance record, stored beside the level, that says which venues were kept, which were dropped and why. Harrowgate's constructed four-venue market at 13:00 is the bench for all of it.

Part 0

One level from four venues, and a reason for every venue left out

Harrowgate is a constructed data team at a fund-administration firm. Every day at 13:00 it has to publish one reference level for an abstract asset that trades on four venues, and every day somebody downstream may ask two questions: how was that number made, and why was a venue left out. The first is a method question. The second is a records question, and it is the one that hurts, because a level without a record of its exclusions cannot be reproduced and cannot be defended. The market on this page is generated by a seeded engine and declared in full: four venues following one constructed price path, each with its own trade rate and basis, one of them frozen since 10:00. No asset, no venue name, no currency.

★ What you walk out with tonight. A venue quote as four numbers rather than one. Four estimators as code, and the reason they land 75.5 ticks apart on the same four quotes. The weighted median as a cumulative-volume search you can do by hand. Two exclusion rules and the blind spot of each. The ten-partition, time-weighted fixing from one public index rulebook, including what an empty partition does and when the level is simply not published. And a provenance record, the small file that turns a number into something you can defend.

LiveWhy the record matters as much as the method3 min▶

A reference level is read by people who were not in the room when it was made: an auditor, a client, a colleague reconciling a valuation three weeks later. Each of them can recompute a mean if you tell them the inputs. None of them can recompute "we left venue D out because it looked wrong", because "looked wrong" is not a rule and the inputs that made it look wrong were never kept.

So the job has two outputs, always. The level, and a record beside it: the method, the window, the partitions, the venues that contributed, the venues that did not and the rule that excluded each one. The second output is cheap to write at the moment of computation and nearly impossible to reconstruct afterwards.

Real world

Harrowgate's first fixing job wrote one row a day: date, level. In its third month a client asked why one Tuesday's level sat well below a venue they watched. The answer was that the venue had been excluded that day as an outlier, which was correct, but nothing recorded it, the venue's quotes had not been kept, and the team spent two days re-running the job from raw prints to prove a decision it had made in a millisecond. Harrowgate is constructed; the two lost days are a common cost.

Self-studyThe words that keep coming back4 min▶
  • Venue quote. What one venue says at a moment: its last print at or before the moment, how old that print is, and how many trades and how much volume it printed in a trailing window.
  • Window. The trailing interval the volume and trade count are measured over. Five minutes in Part 1; one hour in the partitioned fixing.
  • Age. Seconds since the venue's last print. It measures when the venue last traded, not when its price last moved.
  • Basis. A venue's persistent offset from the market path. Venue B sits 40 ticks above it, venue C 30 below, in the constructed market.
  • Estimator. The rule that turns several quotes into one level: mean, median, volume-weighted mean (VWAP), volume-weighted median.
  • Exclusion rule. A test that removes a quote before the estimator runs: too old, or too far from the median.
  • Partition. A slice of the calculation window with its own representative value. Ten slices of 6 minutes in one public index rulebook.
  • Time weight. A weight that grows with recency, 1/10 for the oldest partition up to 10/10 for the newest, normalised before use.
  • Provenance record. The method, window, partitions and the kept and dropped venues with reasons, stored beside the level.
Part 1 · covers quotes, windows and four estimators

Quotes, windows and four estimators

14 min live

Before an estimator can run, each venue has to be reduced to a quote, and the reduction is already a set of choices: which moment, which window, what counts as the venue's price. At 13:00 with a five-minute window, the four constructed venues quote 99.670, 99.706, 99.637 and 99.969, and the constructed market path itself sits at 99.665. Four estimators then turn those four quotes into four levels, and the distance between the highest and the lowest is 75.5 ticks before anything has been excluded.

LiveA venue quote is four numbers, not one5 min▶

The engine's quote for a venue at a moment walks the venue's tape up to that second and keeps four things: the price of the last print at or before the moment, the age of that print in seconds, the number of trades in the trailing window and their summed size. At 13:00 with a five-minute window, the constructed market gives:

  • Venue A, 3,000 trades a day, no basis: last print 99.670, 12 seconds old, 13 trades and 249.101 units in the window.
  • Venue B, 1,500 a day, 40 ticks above the path: 99.706, 80 seconds old, 4 trades, 207.500 units.
  • Venue C, 600 a day, 30 ticks below: 99.637, 73 seconds old, 2 trades, 35.653 units.
  • Venue D, 250 a day, its price frozen since 10:00: 99.969, 392 seconds old, no trades and no volume in the window.

Keep all four numbers. The price alone hides that D has not printed for 392 seconds and that C's "price" rests on two trades. Each estimator below reads a different subset of the four, and the exclusion rules in Part 2 read the age and the distance. A quote table with only a price column cannot support either.

LiveFour estimators, and the column each one reads5 min▶

The simple mean reads only the price column and gives each venue a quarter: 99.7455. D's frozen 99.969 pulls it far above three venues that sit close together.

The median also reads only prices, but sorts them and takes the middle. With four quotes there is no single middle, so it averages the second and third, A and B: 99.688. D can no longer drag it by its distance, only by its position in the sort.

The volume-weighted mean reads price and volume, and weights each price by the venue's window volume: 99.6828. D carries zero volume and so zero weight. B's 207.500 units pull the level above A's quote.

The volume-weighted median sorts by price and walks up the running volume until it passes half, then takes the price where that happened: 99.670, A's quote, because A's block holds the halfway mark. That is the figure above. Four defensible methods, four levels, 75.5 ticks apart, and the two volume-weighted medians sit closest to the path's 99.665 only because, at this minute, volume and honesty happen to point the same way.

Self-studyThe weighted median by hand, and its two edge cases4 min▶

The procedure is three lines. Sort the quotes by price. Accumulate their volumes in that order. Return the price of the first quote at which twice the running total reaches or exceeds the grand total. On the constructed quotes the walk goes C, then A, and the running total passes half inside A, so the answer is A's price. B and D are never reached.

Edge case one: exactly half. If the running total lands exactly on half at the end of a block, the engine's rule ("reaches or exceeds") returns the lower price; another implementation might average the two neighbours. Neither is wrong; an undocumented one is. Write the tie rule into the provenance record.

Edge case two: no volume at all. If every venue in the window is silent, the running total is zero and a weighted median is undefined. The engine falls back to the lowest-priced quote for the weighted median and to the plain median for the weighted mean, which keeps a widget from crashing but is not a policy. The rulebook in Part 2 has a better answer: the level is not published.

The leader view of these same four quotes, with the methods drawn as balance beams, is in Leader session 3, one asset, many prices. Here the interest is the mechanics underneath.

Part 2 · covers exclusion rules and partitions

Exclusion rules and partitions

13 min live

An estimator is only as good as the quotes it is allowed to see. Two exclusion rules come first: drop a quote whose last print is more than 600 seconds old, and drop a quote more than 0.2 percent from the median. Then the larger move, from one public index rulebook: stop using anybody's last print, pool the trades of an hour, cut the hour into ten partitions and weight them toward the fixing time. Each step removes a failure and introduces a rule that has to be written down.

LiveTwo rules, and which one can see the frozen venue4 min▶

Venue D's price stopped following the market at 10:00. It is the obvious candidate for exclusion, and the obvious rule, staleness, does not catch it. D's last print is 392 seconds old at 13:00, inside the 600-second limit, because D is still trading. It is trading at a price that no longer moves. Age measures when a venue last printed, not when its price last meant anything.

The distance rule catches it. The median of the four quotes is 99.688 and D sits 0.28 percent above it, outside the 0.2 percent tolerance, so D is dropped as an outlier. With D gone the four estimators read 99.671, 99.670, 99.6828 and 99.670. The volume-weighted mean did not move at all: D had no volume to weight, so it was never in that estimator to begin with.

Notice what the distance rule measured against: a median that includes D itself. With four quotes that is harmless. With three venues, one of them frozen, the frozen quote is one third of its own reference, and a tolerance that works at four venues may fail at three. Record the rule, the reference it used and the venue count on the day.

LiveWash venue B, and watch which estimator notices4 min▶

Tick the wash box on Tape A. Venue B now reports 60 percent of its volume as matched buy-and-sell pairs that move nothing. Its share of the five-minute window's volume rises to 45.9 percent, against a 24.8 percent share of the day's honest volume across the four venues (45.2 percent of the day's reported volume). The VWAP moves from 99.6828 to 99.6866, toward B's quote, because B's weight is now mostly wash. The volume-weighted median stays at 99.670: B's block grew, but the halfway mark is still inside A's.

That is a narrow escape, not a property. Switch to Tape B with the wash on and the volume-weighted median jumps to venue B's own quote, because B's block now holds the halfway mark. A median resists a distant price; it does not resist a heavy one. Volume weighting is correct when the volume is real, which is why the rulebooks below decide who may contribute before anybody's volume is trusted. Testing whether a venue's reported volume is real starts in session 4.

Real world

Cong, Li, Tang and Yang, "Crypto Wash Trading" (NBER working paper w30783, read at source), estimate on their 2019 sample that "70% of wash trading of total reported volume moves an exchange's rank up by 46 positions" on a third-party ranking site's data. A ranking and a volume weight are the same mechanism: whoever reports more, counts more. The authors present their figures as estimates with stated bounds, and venues may have adapted since.

LiveOne public index rulebook, partition by partition5 min▶

One public index rulebook, read at source, replaces the last-print quote with something much harder to push around. It fixes a 3,600-second calculation window ending at the fixing time and splits it into 10 partitions of 6 minutes. Within each partition, the trades of every selected venue are pooled and sorted by price, and the partition's value is the trade where the running volume passes half: in the rulebook's words, the "most representative trade is defined as the volume median one", which it calls "outlier resistant by nature". It is Figure A's walk, done on individual trades instead of venue quotes, ten times.

The ten partition values are then combined with time weights 1/10 to 10/10, normalised, so the partition just before the fixing counts ten times as much as the first. Three rules handle the gaps. A partition with no trades is excluded and the weights "adjusted accordingly". A partition whose data could not be retrieved in time is excluded too. And if there are no trades in the whole window, "the price is not published". For suspect transactions, "the most representative trade may be adjusted to disregard the spurious data".

What a late partition is, and how a job decides the data for a slice has all arrived, is a watermark question; the mechanics are taught in learn-streaming-data-with-phoebe. Here the point is the policy: late data is excluded and recorded, never quietly used in the next run.

Self-studyThe venue screen comes before any weight4 min▶

The same rulebook decides which venues may contribute at all, quarterly, before a single trade is pooled. The criteria include "Regulated by an independent government body", "KYC/AML controls", "Trading Policies", five years of operation, absence from sanction lists and reliable trade feeds, plus a liquidity floor: a venue must carry at least 0.5 percent of observed liquidity in the pair over the past three months. Exclusion actions include "Fraud", "Market manipulation" and "Significant loss of volume or liquidity".

Read that in the light of the washed venue. A volume median inside a partition is outlier resistant, but it still lets the heaviest venue decide where half the volume lies. The defence against a heavy venue whose weight is fiction is not a cleverer estimator; it is a decision, made in advance and on stated criteria, that the venue does not contribute. A second methodology is reported to work differently in the details: 12 five-minute partitions, a volume-weighted median in each, then an equal-weighted average of the twelve, with medians chosen to reduce "susceptibility to price extremes" on any one venue.

Self-studyWhere serious people disagree3 min▶

One camp weights partitions toward the fixing, as the first rulebook does. The level should describe the market at 13:00, and a partition from 12:00 knows less about 13:00 than one from 12:54. Equal weights, on this view, publish a level that is partly an hour stale.

The other camp weights partitions equally, as the reported second methodology does. A time weight hands the most influence to the last few minutes before a known fixing time, which is exactly when a participant who wants to move the level would act. Equal weights spread that influence across the hour and make the fixing harder to lean on.

Both camps use medians inside partitions, both screen venues first, and both write the weighting into a public document. They disagree on whether recency or resistance to the last-minute push is the failure worth paying for. Whichever you pick, the provenance record names it.

Part 3 · build-along

Build the fixing, then write down what it left out

15 min hands on

Six steps with pandas on constructed trades from four venues, as one CSV with the columns venue, t in seconds, side, size in units and px in ticks. The default market reproduces the quotes above to the last tick; your numbers will differ on any other seed or tape, which is the point. Run it in a scratch folder, never where the outputs could land in a repository.

Reduce each venue to a quote with all four numbers

Last print at or before the moment, its age, and the trade count and volume in the trailing window. Keep the venue's own row order for prints that share a second.

python · venue quotesimport json import numpy as np import pandas as pd trades = pd.read_csv("venues.csv") # venue, t, side, size, px (ticks) trades = trades.sort_values(["venue", "t"], kind="stable") def venue_quote(df, at, window): seen = df[df["t"] <= at] if seen.empty: return dict(px=None, age=None, n=0, vol=0.0) last = seen.iloc[-1] win = seen[seen["t"] > at - window] return dict(px=float(last["px"]), age=float(at - last["t"]), n=len(win), vol=float(win["size"].sum())) AT, WINDOW = 46800, 300 # 13:00, five minutes quotes = pd.DataFrame({v: venue_quote(g, AT, WINDOW) for v, g in trades.groupby("venue")}).T print(quotes)

Write the four estimators side by side

Each one reads a different set of columns. Putting them next to each other is how you see which column a disagreement comes from.

python · four estimatorsdef estimators(q): px, vol = q["px"].astype(float), q["vol"].astype(float) total = vol.sum() return { "mean": px.mean(), "median": px.median(), "vwap": (px * vol).sum() / total if total else None, "vw_median": weighted_median(px, vol), }

The weighted median as a cumulative-volume search

Sort by price, accumulate volume, return the first price where the running total reaches half. The tie rule is the one in the search, so state it.

python · weighted mediandef weighted_median(px, vol): order = np.argsort(np.asarray(px), kind="stable") px, vol = np.asarray(px)[order], np.asarray(vol)[order] cum = np.cumsum(vol) if cum[-1] == 0: return None # no volume: undefined, say so i = np.searchsorted(cum, cum[-1] / 2) # first running total >= half return px[i] print({k: v and v / 1000 for k, v in estimators(quotes).items()})

Masks, not deletions: staleness and distance

Mark each quote with the reason it was dropped instead of filtering it away, so the reason survives into the record.

python · exclusion masksMAX_AGE, TOL = 600, 0.002 med = quotes["px"].astype(float).median() # the reference includes every venue quotes["why"] = "" quotes.loc[quotes["age"].astype(float) > MAX_AGE, "why"] = "stale" far = (quotes["px"].astype(float) - med).abs() / med > TOL quotes.loc[far & (quotes["why"] == ""), "why"] = "outlier" kept = quotes[quotes["why"] == ""] print(quotes[["px", "age", "why"]]) print({k: v and v / 1000 for k, v in estimators(kept).items()})

A ten-partition, time-weighted fixing

Pool the kept venues' trades over the hour, take the volume median trade in each six-minute partition, weight k/10, drop empty or late partitions, renormalise. No partition at all means no level.

python · partitioned fixingdef fixing(trades, venues, end, window=3600, parts=10, late=()): start, width = end - window, window // parts pool = trades[trades["venue"].isin(venues) & (trades["t"] >= start) & (trades["t"] < end)].copy() pool["part"] = (pool["t"] - start) // width rows = [] for k in range(parts): p = pool[pool["part"] == k] why = "late" if k in late else ("empty" if p.empty else "") value = None if why else weighted_median(p["px"], p["size"]) rows.append(dict(part=k, value=value, weight=(k + 1) / parts, why=why)) used = [r for r in rows if not r["why"]] if not used: return None, rows # not published total = sum(r["weight"] for r in used) # renormalise over what is left return sum(r["value"] * r["weight"] for r in used) / total, rows level, parts = fixing(trades, list(kept.index), end=AT)

Store the provenance record beside the level

Method, window, partitions with their reasons, venues kept and dropped with theirs, and the rule parameters. One file per fixing, written in the same run that made the number.

python · provenancerecord = dict( level=None if level is None else round(level / 1000, 4), at=AT, window=3600, partitions_n=10, method="volume median trade per 360 s partition, time weights k/10, renormalised", tie_rule="first price where running volume reaches half", partitions=parts, venues_kept=list(kept.index), venues_dropped={v: w for v, w in quotes["why"].items() if w}, rules=dict(max_age=MAX_AGE, tol=TOL, reference="median of all quotes"), ) with open("fixing_1300.json", "w") as f: json.dump(record, f, indent=2, default=str)

The habit to keep. Never filter a venue out; mark it out, with a reason, and carry the mark into a file that sits beside the level. The estimator is a choice you can defend in a sentence. The exclusion is a choice you can only defend with the record.

Check yourself

Three questions

Venue D is frozen at 99.969 and the simple mean reads 99.7455. Why does the volume-weighted median stay at 99.670 without any exclusion rule?

A weighted median only listens to weight. D has a price and no volume in the window, so it cannot move the point where the running total passes half. No age test is built into the estimator, and the averaging of the middle two is what the plain median does, giving 99.688.

In the partitioned fixing, the 12:18 partition has no trades. What happens to it?

The rulebook excludes an empty partition and adjusts the weights accordingly. Carrying a value forward would publish a number nobody traded, and a zero is not a price. Only when every partition is empty does the rule change: then the price is not published at all.

Why does the venue screen come before any volume weighting?

Volume weighting is correct when the volume is real. On Tape A with B washed, the VWAP moved toward B; on Tape B the volume-weighted median jumped to B's quote. No estimator can tell real volume from reported volume, so the rulebooks decide who contributes, on stated criteria, before anybody is weighted.
