The tape bench

Nine sessions took the problem apart a piece at a time: the tape, the candles, the venues, the first-digit law, roundness and clustering, the planted washer, the calibration, the depeg and the join. This one puts all of it in front of you with every input exposed. Two constructed venues, one honest and one with a wash trader whose share of reported volume you set. Three detectors read the size column and nothing else, judged against a band measured on forty honest venues, with the false-positive rate on two hundred more printed beside the verdict. Then the two controls that make a bench honest: a lever that only ever makes the number worse, and a button that dissolves the structure the detectors depend on. Nothing here is a real market and nothing here is investment advice.

★ What you walk out with today. Three measured numbers you can quote from memory, each obtained by moving a control rather than by being told: the share of reported volume at which a naive washer is first flagged, the share of honest venues flagged with it, and the weight a washed venue reaches when a table is ranked by what venues say about themselves. A feel for which inputs move the verdict and which do nothing. And a short written brief for any self-reported number, in the form somebody else could check.

LiveWhat the bench recomputes on every press4 min▶

On load the engine generates forty honest tapes and measures the mean and spread of each detector on them; that is the calibration, and it never sees a washed tape. It then generates two hundred more honest tapes, never used for calibration, and counts how many of them the rule flags at the sensitivity you chose; that is the false-positive rate, and it is a measurement rather than a promise. Then, on every press, it generates venue A honest and venue B with the planted washer, runs the three detectors on each, judges them against the band, and prints the verdict beside the volumes.

The washer is declared, not hidden. It trades in matched pairs, a buy and a sell of the same size one to four seconds apart, until its volume reaches the share you set of the venue's reported total. The round bot cycles seven round lots. The flat bot draws unrounded four-digit sizes from one narrow band. The copy bot samples the honest size distribution, and it exists so that the page can say out loud what these detectors cannot see.

LiveThe five runs worth doing first6 min▶
  • Leave everything alone. Pair A, 30 percent, round bot, k=2. Venue A reads a first-digit deviation of 0.0065, a round share of 29.0 percent and a cluster share of 11.5 percent, all inside the band; nothing fires. Venue B reads 0.0507, 67.0 and 53.2; all three fire and the venue is flagged. Under the table: 3 of 200 honest venues are flagged at this sensitivity, 1.5 percent, and the first-digit test alone fires on 13 of them.
  • Drag the share down to 2 percent. Round share and cluster share still fire, 32.9 and 14.9 against bands that end at 31.6 and 13.5, and the venue is flagged. The first-digit test is silent at 0.0064 and stays silent until 5 percent, because round lots keep first digits 1, 2 and 5, which Benford already favours. At 1 percent nothing fires. That is the floor on a naive bot.
  • Switch to the flat bot at 30 percent. Round share falls to 12.4 percent and cluster share to 5.0, both BELOW the band, and both fire anyway because the tests are two-sided. This is the direction the published study found on unregulated venues: too little roundness, not too much. A one-sided test would have called this venue honest.
  • Switch to the copy bot and drag the share to 70 percent. Round share 30.7, cluster 12.6, first-digit 0.0074. Nothing fires at any share. The washer is now invisible to every size-based test by construction, and the only thing left on the tape is the pairing, which these three detectors do not read.
  • Raise k to 4 with the round bot at 30 percent. The honest false-positive rate drops to 0 of 200 and the verdict on venue B does not change. Then try k=4 on the flat bot at 5 percent: two detectors go quiet. Sensitivity buys fewer false alarms with a higher floor, and the trade is printed, not assumed.
Self-studyWhy the copy bot is the honest limit, not a failure4 min▶

Every detector on this bench is a statement about the distribution of trade sizes. A washer that draws its sizes from the honest distribution produces a tape whose size distribution is, by construction, the honest one, and no amount of sensitivity recovers a signal that is not there. The copy bot is on the bench so that the page can say this out loud rather than letting a reader infer from four flagged rungs that the detector catches washers in general. It catches naive washers. The published study caught naive washers too, and its authors say so in their caveats.

What the copy bot leaves on the tape is time structure: a buy and a sell of the same size one to four seconds apart, over and over. A detector that reads timestamps and sides as well as sizes would see it, and session 7's homework builds one. It would also see honest traders who happen to reverse a position quickly, which is why it needs the same calibration against honest venues before anybody trusts it. The pattern is general: every detector has a thing it reads, a thing it cannot read, and a false-positive rate that has to be measured rather than hoped.

Self-studyThe direction the published study found4 min▶

The study that introduced these three tests examined 29 venues over four months of 2019 and found that the regulated ones clustered at round sizes, with round trades around 30 percent of the total, while the unregulated ones mostly did not: "30% and 50% of exchanges displaying no trade-size clustering" among the two unregulated tiers. Its quantification rests on exactly that direction: a benchmark ratio of unrounded to round volume from the regulated venues, and any unrounded volume above what that ratio predicts is counted as wash. The authors' estimate was an average of 77.5 percent of reported volume on unregulated venues, with a median of 79.1, and they describe the Benford-based version as a lower bound and the roundness-based version as possibly an upper bound if authentic algorithmic trading inflates unrounded volume.

The bench's round bot is therefore a different animal from the one in the study: the naive script that pumps volume in whole lots, which is the first thing anybody would write. The flat bot is closer to what the study found. The two-sided rule exists so that both are caught, and the self test's fifth property exists because a one-sided implementation passed every other check and would have shipped.

Part 2 · the lever and the button

A lever that only makes it worse, and a button that dissolves the structure

14 min hands on

A bench that can only report a better number is a demonstration, not a measurement. Two controls make this one honest. The anti-lever does the obvious thing every league table does, weight venues by the volume they report, and the washed venue's weight climbs with its wash share. The break button pools every trade size on both venues and deals them back out at random, so the planted structure is spread evenly and the detectors should stop telling the venues apart.

LiveRank the venues by their reported volume5 min▶

Switch the anti-lever on with Pair A at 70 percent. The table re-sorts and venue B sits at rank 1 with 78.8 percent of the pair's reported volume. Its honest weight is 52.7 percent, the same at every share, because adding a washer does not change a single honest print. The extra 26 points of weight are the washer's volume being counted as if it were a measurement of anything.

Drag the share back to 0 and the weight returns to 52.7. Try the other pairs: on Pairs B and C the washed venue moves from rank 2 to rank 1 at 10 percent wash, on Pairs D and E at 30 percent. The lever never improves the table, on any pair, at any share above zero. That is what makes it a lever worth having on a bench: it demonstrates that the obvious policy is the trap, with a number rather than a warning.

The nuance the page owes you: weighting by volume is standard practice and it is correct when the volume is real. Public index rulebooks weight by volume inside their partitions. They can afford to because they screen which venues may contribute at all, quarterly, on published criteria, and they drop a venue for fraud or market manipulation. The lesson is not "never weight by volume"; it is "never weight by a volume whose reporter has not been screened".

LiveShuffle the sizes and watch the gap go5 min▶

Set Pair A to 50 percent with the round bot, read the two venues' round shares, 29.0 and 80.2 percent, then press the break button. Every trade size on both venues goes into one pool and is dealt back out at random. Now both venues read about 55 percent, the gap between them is -0.1 points, and the cluster and first-digit gaps are +0.7 points and -0.0001. Both venues are flagged, 3 of 3, because the pooled tape is half wash and the detectors say so.

Read what that proves carefully, because it is easy to over-read. The shuffle proves that the detectors measure the distribution of sizes and that the venue label carries no information of its own: once the sizes are mixed, the venues are indistinguishable. It does not make the detectors read honest, and it should not, because the wash is still on the tape. A break button that made everything read clean would be proving that the detectors measure nothing. Press it at 0 percent and both venues stay honest; the button only ever moves the gap, never the level.

Self-studyTwo published estimates that disagree, and why both are kept4 min▶

An industry presentation to a regulator in 2019, reported rather than read at source because the hosted document is image-only, estimated that roughly 95 percent of the volume one widely used aggregator reported for one asset was fake or non-economic, across 81 venues over four days, with 10 venues found to carry real volume. The academic study the detectors come from estimated 77.5 percent of reported volume on unregulated venues, across 26 unregulated venues over four months, with the regulated venues at roughly zero.

The two numbers have different denominators, samples, windows and methods, and the academic authors describe their own figure as bounded on both sides. A data team that picks one and quotes it as "the wash rate" has made the same mistake as a team that quotes a venue's own volume: it has promoted a claim to a measurement. The honest sentence keeps both, with their samples, and says they disagree. Leader session 2 is built on that sentence.

Part 3 · the brief

What you write down

11 min live

Every control on the bench points at the same discipline: a self-reported number has to arrive with the thing that bounds it. Ten minutes, on a number you actually publish.

State who reported the number and what they gain if it is high

Not "volume" but "volume as reported by the venue itself, which is ranked by it". One sentence. If nobody gains from the number being high, say so; if somebody does, the number is a target and will behave like one.

State the detector, its band, and its false-positive rate

"Three size-distribution tests, two-sided on roundness and clustering, flagged on two of three, band from 40 honest venues at k=2, 1.5 percent of 200 honest venues flagged." If you cannot fill the last clause, you do not have a detector, you have an opinion with a threshold.

State what the detector cannot see

"A washer that copies the honest size distribution is invisible to these tests; the matched-pair structure is not read." Write the blind spot before anybody asks, because the first person to find it will otherwise be the one you least want to.

Say what would change your mind

A screened venue list from a published rulebook. A second detector that reads timestamps and sides, calibrated the same way. A newer peer-reviewed estimate than the 2019 sample. Write the trigger down now, while nothing is on fire.

LiveThe three numbers, on one card3 min▶

The floor. A round-lot washer is flagged from 2 percent of reported volume at k=2, and the floor does not move at k=2.5 or 3; a flat bot is flagged from 4 percent at k=2, 5 at 2.5 and 6 at 3. A copy bot is never flagged at any share up to 70 percent.

The false alarm. 3 of 200 honest venues are flagged at k=2, 1.5 percent; 0 of 200 at k=2.5 and above, which is a ceiling statement about 200 trials and not a zero. The first-digit test alone fires on 6.5 percent of honest venues at k=2, which is why the rule needs two of three.

The trap. Weight by reported volume and the washed venue's weight is 78.8 percent at 70 percent wash against an honest 52.7, and it takes rank 1 on every seed pair. The lever never improves anything, and the rulebooks avoid the trap by screening the reporter, not by abandoning the weight.

After the course

Homework

About an hour, on something real.

Find one self-reported number in your own estate

Anything a source publishes about itself and your team quotes: a vendor's row count, a partner's active users, a venue's volume. Write down who gains if it is high. If the answer is "the reporter", you have found a tape.

Calibrate one detector you already run

Pick any threshold alert in a pipeline you own and measure how often it fires on data you know to be clean. If you have never measured it, that number is the finding, and it is the first line of the brief above.

Write the blind spot down

For the same detector, describe the input that would pass it while being exactly what it exists to catch. Every detector has one. The copy bot is the one on this bench; yours is somewhere in the thing your detector does not read.

Check yourself

Three questions

At k=2 the bench flags 3 of 200 honest venues. A colleague proposes k=4, where 0 of 200 are flagged, and calls the detector "now false-positive free". What is wrong?

A · Nothing; a higher k is strictly better because it removes false alarms
B · The honest band should be measured on washed tapes instead
C · 0 of 200 is a ceiling statement about 200 trials, not a zero rate, and the same k raises the floor on a flat bot, which the bench prints
A measured rate of zero on 200 trials bounds the true rate; it does not make it zero. Sensitivity is a trade, and the bench prints both sides of it: fewer false alarms and a higher wash share before a flat bot is seen. Calibrating on washed tapes would make the band a property of the thing being looked for, which is the fault calibration exists to avoid.

After the break button, both venues read 3 of 3 at 50 percent wash. What does that show?

A · The detectors measure the size distribution and the venue label carries no information once sizes are mixed; the wash is still on the tape, so both read contaminated
B · The detectors are broken, because a shuffle should make everything read honest
C · The shuffle moved the wash trades to venue A
A shuffle conserves every size; the self test checks that the total is unchanged. What vanishes is the gap between the venues, not the contamination of the pool. A break button that made the pool read honest would be evidence that the detectors measured nothing.

The copy bot at 70 percent wash fires nothing. Which sentence belongs in a report that uses these detectors?

A · The venue's volume has been verified as genuine
B · A washer that samples the honest size distribution is invisible to these size-based tests; the matched-pair structure is not read by them
C · The detector needs a higher k to catch it
No k recovers a signal that is not in the thing the detector reads. The copy bot's tape has the honest size distribution by construction. The honest sentence names the blind spot and the structure a different detector would have to read, which is exactly what the published authors do in their own caveats.
