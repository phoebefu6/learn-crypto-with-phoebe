Planting a wash trader

Sessions 4 and 5 built three detectors and pointed them at a bot. Here is the question nobody asked out loud: how do you know the bot was a bot? On a real venue nobody can tell you which trades were wash, so nobody can tell you how often a detector is right. The honest way out is to build the answer key first. Tonight you plant a wash trader into a constructed tape on purpose, write down exactly what you planted, check that the plant is what you said it is, and lock the label away where no detector can read it. Everything after this, every detection rate and every false alarm, is counted against that key.

Part 0

Harrowgate has three detectors and no way to mark their homework

Harrowgate is a constructed data team at a fund-administration firm. After session 5 it has a first-digit test, a roundness test and a clustering test, and a manager who asks the reasonable question: how good are they? The team's first instinct is to run them on real venues and count the flags. That counts flags, not hits. To know whether a flag was right, someone has to know which trades on that venue were wash, and on a real venue nobody outside the washer does. The only ground truth Harrowgate can ever have is one it planted itself, on a tape it generated, with the plant written down before any detector runs.

LiveCounting flags is not measuring a detector3 min▶

A detector has two numbers that matter: how often it fires on a tape that has wash in it, and how often it fires on a tape that does not. Both are ratios with a known answer in the denominator. "Flagged 4 of 20 venues" has no known answer anywhere; it could be four hits, four false alarms, or two of each, and nothing on the tapes can tell you which.

So the work splits into two jobs that must never touch. One job builds tapes and knows what is in them. The other job runs detectors and is not allowed to know. Tonight is the first job, done carefully enough that session 7 can do the second.

Real world

Harrowgate's first draft of a venue screen reported "detector agreement" as its quality metric: when all three tests fired on a venue, the screen was assumed to be right. A reviewer pointed out that three tests built on the same size column can agree perfectly and all be wrong in the same way. The team replaced agreement with a planted bench, and the agreement metric went into the appendix. Harrowgate is constructed; the shortcut is common.

Self-studyThe words that keep coming back4 min▶
  • Wash trade. A buy and a sell of the same size that leave the trader where they started. It adds to reported volume and moves no risk.
  • Pair, or twin. The two legs of one wash trade: same size, opposite side, a few seconds apart. On this bench, 1 to 4 seconds.
  • Honest volume. The sum of sizes of the trades that are not wash. Fixed when the honest tape is generated; planting never changes it.
  • Reported volume. The sum of sizes of every print, honest and wash. The number a venue publishes.
  • Wash share, w. Wash volume divided by reported volume. A share of volume, not a share of trades.
  • Ground truth. The correct label for every trade, known independently of any detector.
  • Declared structure. The written description of what was planted, how much and in what style, fixed before any detector runs.
  • Test harness. The code and files that compare a detector's output with the declared labels. The only place the labels are allowed to live.
Part 1 · covers why ground truth must be planted

Why the tape must be synthetic, and declared

14 min live

There is no labelled public set of wash trades. The published estimates of how much wash there is are models, built carefully, with their authors' own bounds printed beside them. A planted structure is the one thing that gives a detector a known answer: you decide what is wash, you write it down, and then detection rate and false positives stop being opinions and become counts.

LiveNobody holds the answer key for a real venue5 min▶

A real tape has the columns in the middle of the figure and no others. Time, side, size, price. There is no wash column, because the only party who knows which prints were wash is the party who placed them, and that party is not filling in a spreadsheet for you. A venue that inflates its volume has no reason to label it; a venue that does not inflate has no way to prove a negative.

That makes every real-data accuracy claim circular. If you score a detector by whether it flags the venues you already suspected, you are measuring agreement with your suspicion. If you score it against another detector, you are measuring agreement between two instruments that may share a blind spot. Neither is a detection rate. There is no labelled public ground truth for wash trades to check either against.

The generator in the figure removes the circle by construction. The builder writes the wash flag at the moment each pair is created, so the label is a fact about how the tape was made, not a judgement about how it looks. Every widget on these pages says the tape is synthetic for the same reason: a number measured on a planted tape is only honest if the reader knows it was planted.

LiveWhat the published estimates actually are4 min▶

The best-known academic estimate is a model, and its authors say so. Cong, Li, Tang and Yang, "Crypto Wash Trading" (NBER working paper read at source; published version in Management Science, reported), estimated a benchmark ratio of unrounded to round volume on three regulated venues, then treated an unregulated venue's unrounded volume above what that ratio predicts as the wash estimate. The answer depends on the benchmark being right, and no trade in their sample carries a label saying it was wash.

Real world

On their 2019 sample of 29 venues the authors estimate wash at "77.5% of the total trading volume on unregulated exchanges" on average, and they print the bounds beside it: their Benford-based estimates "are significantly lower" and give "somewhat lower bounds", and if genuine algorithmic trading inflates unrounded volume "our estimates should be viewed more as upper bounds". They add "we cannot assert that our estimates are the gold standard". That is what careful work without ground truth looks like: an estimate, its method, and its bounds, side by side.

None of this is a criticism of the paper. It is the reason a detector cannot be validated on real tapes: if the best estimate is a model with a lower and an upper bound, there is nothing more exact to score a detector against.

LiveWhat a planted structure buys you5 min▶

Once you planted the wash, two ratios become arithmetic. Detection rate: of the tapes you washed, the share the detector flagged. False-positive rate: of the tapes you left honest, the share it flagged anyway. Both have a known denominator because you built it. Session 7 computes both on the bench, including the floor, the smallest wash share each bot can hide at.

The price is scope. A planted tape only tests the bots you thought to plant. A detector that catches your round-lot bot every time has learned nothing about a washer you did not imagine, and Part 2 plants one on purpose, a bot that copies the honest size habit, to keep that limit in view. A planted bench is a lower standard than the truth and a higher one than a guess.

Declaring the structure is what stops the bench being tuned. Write down the wash share, the style, the seeds and the pair rule before any detector runs, and the result cannot drift toward whatever the detector happened to find.

Self-studyWhere serious people disagree4 min▶

Two published numbers are often quoted as if they measured the same thing. Cong and co-authors estimate 77.5 percent on average for unregulated venues: an academic roundness method, 26 unregulated venues against 3 regulated ones, four months in 2019. An industry presentation to the US securities regulator in March 2019 (Fusaro and Hougan, an asset manager's presentation, reported; the hosted file is image-only) reported that roughly 95 percent of the volume shown on a widely used aggregator for one asset was "fake or non-economic", from 81 venues over four days, using size histograms, volume patterns and spread behaviour, and found 10 venues with real volume. The academic authors say their estimates "are in the same order of magnitude".

Different denominators, different samples, different windows, different methods. Neither is "the" wash rate, and neither could be checked against a label, because no label exists. That is the strongest argument for a planted bench: it is the only setting where a disagreement about a detector can be settled by counting.

The opposing view deserves its due. A practitioner who works on real venues will say a synthetic bench tests the builder's imagination, and that the only detector worth trusting is one that has been argued over on real tapes by people with different priors. Both camps are right about the other's weakness. The bench measures what it planted; real tapes measure nothing exactly.

Part 2 · covers the declared structure

Pairs, a share of reported volume, and three habits

13 min live

The declaration has four parts. A wash trade is a pair: one buy and one sell of the same size, 1 to 4 seconds apart. The wash share w is a share of reported volume. The bot keeps adding pairs until wash volume reaches w / (1 - w) of honest volume, which is exactly the amount that makes wash divided by reported equal w. And the bot has one size habit, chosen from three. The honest tape underneath never changes: on venue B, the partner of Tape A, it is 3,000 trades and 105,370.65 units on every rung.

Livew is a share of reported volume, not of trades4 min▶

If honest volume is H and the bot adds wash volume W, the venue reports H + W, and the wash share is W / (H + W). Set that equal to w and solve: W = H × w / (1 - w). So at 10 percent the bot adds wash worth a ninth of honest volume, at 50 percent it adds as much as the honest traders did, and at 70 percent it adds more than twice as much. The generator keeps adding pairs until wash volume reaches that target; nothing else stops it.

Venue B, round-lot bot, the honest tape fixed at 3,000 trades and 105,370.65 units:

Read the trade column against the declared share. At 70 percent of volume, 18,312 of the 21,312 trades are wash and only 3,000 are honest: the share of trades runs far ahead of the share of volume, because a round-lot bot's average size and an honest trader's are not the same. Say "share of reported volume" every time; a reader who hears "70 percent wash" and pictures 70 percent of trades has the wrong tape in mind.

LiveWhy a pair nets to zero and still counts twice3 min▶

Look at the first bracket in the figure. At 00:01:36 the washer sells 50 units; at 00:01:37 it buys 50 units. Its position after the second leg is exactly where it was before the first. No risk moved to anyone, and on this engine the price does not move either: wash prints are stamped at the current price and only honest prints step it. Yet the reported volume counter added 100 units, because volume is a sum of sizes and it has no column for intent.

That is the whole economics of a wash trade in one line: double the size in volume, zero in position. It is also why the planted structure is a property of pairs, not of single prints. A single 50-unit sell is indistinguishable from an honest one; it is the twin, same size, opposite side, seconds away, that makes it wash. The engine checks every wash trade has its twin; the widget above counts the ones that do not, and the count is 0 on every tape, style and share it offers.

LiveThree styles, three habits3 min▶
  • Round. Every size is one of seven lots, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 units. The naive bot. It pushes roundness and clustering up and keeps first digits on 1, 2 and 5, which is why session 4 found the first-digit test the last to see it.
  • Flat. Unrounded four-digit sizes drawn from one band, 2 to 20 units. Too few round sizes, the direction the published study found on unregulated venues. Session 5's two-sided tests exist for this bot.
  • Copy. Samples the honest size distribution. Its sizes are the honest habit, so every size-based detector is blind to it by construction; only the pair structure gives it away. Planting it is not cruelty to the detectors; it is the declared limit of what they can see.

Switch the widget between styles at the same share and watch the first five pairs: round lots, then four-digit sizes like nobody types, then sizes that look exactly like the honest column. On the copy bot the achieved share can land noticeably above the declared one, because a copied size can be very large and the loop only stops after the pair that crosses the target. The widget prints the achieved share for that reason.

LiveThe checks that keep the plant honest3 min▶

A generator that does not do what its declaration says produces a bench that measures the bug. The engine runs six properties on every page load and the widget footer reports them. Two are about the plant itself: "reported volume equals honest plus wash, share within one lot of target", which catches a wash loop that stops early or double counts, and "every wash trade has an opposite-side twin within 4 seconds", which catches a pair generator that forgets to flip the side. Each of the six names the fault it exists to catch.

A check is only worth something if it can fail. Two faults were reintroduced in node to confirm the right property fails: the twin-side fault, under which the opposite-side twin property fails, and a one-sided roundness test, under which "a bot with too few round sizes still fires the roundness test" fails while the other five pass. The other properties each name the fault they are there for. Session 10 runs all six beside the bench.

Self-studyIntegers, so every check is exact3 min▶

Sizes are stored as integers in micro-units, 1,000,000 to one unit, as Harrowgate has stored them since session 1. That turns the volume identity from "close enough" into an equality: reported volume is honest plus wash to the last micro-unit, and an assertion with no tolerance either holds or names a bug. A float sum over twenty thousand prints can disagree with itself in the last digit depending on the order you add, and a check that needs a tolerance is a check you can talk yourself past.

The share is the one place a tolerance is honest. Pairs come in whole sizes, so the bot cannot stop exactly on the target; it stops on the first pair that reaches it. The overshoot is at most one pair, which for the round bot is within one lot. Write the tolerance as that bound, not as a round number you picked.

The tape is also a stream in time order, and in production it arrives late and out of order; the mechanics of that live in learn-streaming-data-with-phoebe. Here the generator sorts by time once and is done.

Part 3 · build-along

Build a declared tape and lock the label away

15 min hands on

Six steps with numpy and pandas. You generate an honest tape the way the engine does, plant matched pairs to a declared share, assert the two properties, and split the result into a published tape and a label file. Your random draws will differ from the engine's, so your numbers will differ; the comments quote only the constructed bench's figures for comparison. Run it in a scratch folder, never where the outputs could land in a repository.

Honest sizes, typed like a person, in integer micro-units

Log-normal with a wide spread, then rounded to one, two or three significant digits. The rounding is integer arithmetic, so a size is never 999.9999 of anything.

python · one honest sizeimport numpy as np, pandas as pd MICRO = 1_000_000 # micro-units: 0.831 units is 831000 rng = np.random.default_rng(12) # any seed; your numbers will differ from the page def round_sig(raw_micro: int, sig: int) -> int: """Round a positive integer to sig significant digits, integers only.""" raw_micro = max(int(raw_micro), 1) shift = len(str(raw_micro)) - sig # how many trailing digits to drop if shift <= 0: return raw_micro q = 10 ** shift return (raw_micro + q // 2) // q * q def honest_size() -> int: raw = int(np.exp(1.6 + 1.9 * rng.standard_normal()) * MICRO) r = rng.random() sig = 1 if r < 0.25 else 2 if r < 0.70 else 3 # 25 percent one digit, 45 two, the rest three return round_sig(raw, sig)

The honest tape, and its volume, fixed before any wash exists

Record honest volume now. Planting must never change it, and step 4 checks that it did not.

python · 3,000 honest printsN, DAY = 3000, 86_400 honest = pd.DataFrame({ "t": rng.integers(0, DAY, N), "side": rng.choice(["buy", "sell"], N), "size_u": [honest_size() for _ in range(N)], }) honest["wash"] = 0 honest_vol = int(honest["size_u"].sum()) print(N, "honest trades,", honest_vol / MICRO, "units") # constructed venue B: 3,000 trades, 105,370.65 units

Plant matched pairs until wash reaches w / (1 - w) of honest volume

Each pair is two rows: same size, opposite side, 1 to 4 seconds apart, both flagged. The loop stops on the first pair that reaches the target.

python · the declared plantLOTS = [1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100] def wash_size(style: str) -> int: if style == "round": return int(rng.choice(LOTS)) * MICRO # one habit: seven round lots if style == "flat": return round_sig(int((2 + 18 * rng.random()) * MICRO), 4) # four digits, 2 to 20 units return honest_size() # copy: the honest habit itself def plant(w: float, style: str) -> pd.DataFrame: target = honest_vol * w / (1 - w) # so that wash / reported == w rows, wash_vol = [], 0 while wash_vol < target: t, size = int(rng.integers(0, DAY)), wash_size(style) first = str(rng.choice(["buy", "sell"])) second = "sell" if first == "buy" else "buy" gap = int(rng.integers(1, 5)) # 1 to 4 seconds rows += [(t, first, size, 1), (t + gap, second, size, 1)] wash_vol += 2 * size wash = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["t", "side", "size_u", "wash"]) return pd.concat([honest, wash]).sort_values("t", kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True) W, STYLE = 0.10, "round" # the declaration, written before any detector runs tape = plant(W, STYLE) print(len(tape), "trades,", int(tape["wash"].sum()) // 2, "pairs") # constructed venue B at 10 percent: 3,848 trades, 424 pairs

Assert the volume identity and the share

No tolerance on the identity, because the sizes are integers. On the share, the only honest tolerance is the largest pair, because the loop can overshoot by at most one.

python · reported is honest plus washis_wash = tape["wash"] == 1 honest_part = int(tape.loc[~is_wash, "size_u"].sum()) wash_part = int(tape.loc[is_wash, "size_u"].sum()) reported = int(tape["size_u"].sum()) assert reported == honest_part + wash_part # exact assert honest_part == honest_vol # planting never touched an honest row biggest_pair = 2 * int(tape.loc[is_wash, "size_u"].max()) share = wash_part / reported assert -1e-12 <= share - W <= biggest_pair / reported # overshoot at most one pair print(honest_part / MICRO, "+", wash_part / MICRO, "=", reported / MICRO, round(share, 4)) # constructed: 105,370.65 honest, 117,086.65 reported

Assert every wash trade has its twin, and the washer nets to zero

This is the check that fails if the pair generator forgets to flip the side. Prove it to yourself: change second to equal first in step 3 and run again.

python · the twin propertylegs = tape[is_wash] unmatched = 0 for _, r in legs.iterrows(): twins = legs[(legs["size_u"] == r["size_u"]) & (legs["side"] != r["side"]) & ((legs["t"] - r["t"]).abs() <= 4)] unmatched += int(twins.empty) assert unmatched == 0, f"{unmatched} wash trades have no opposite-side twin" signed = np.where(legs["side"] == "buy", 1, -1) * legs["size_u"] assert int(signed.sum()) == 0 # the washer ends where it started print("every wash trade has its twin; net position 0") # constructed bench: 0 unmatched on every tape, style and share

Split the published tape from the declared label

The detector gets a file with no wash column. The label goes to the harness folder with a trade id to join on, and nothing in the detector code is allowed to open that folder.

python · two files, one of them secretfrom pathlib import Path harness = Path("harness"); harness.mkdir(exist_ok=True) tape["trade_id"] = tape.index tape[["trade_id", "t", "side", "size_u"]].to_csv("venue_b_tape.csv", index=False) # what a detector reads tape[["trade_id", "wash"]].to_csv(harness / "declared_labels.csv", index=False) # the answer key (harness / "declaration.txt").write_text(f"w={W} style={STYLE} pairs={int(is_wash.sum()) // 2}\n") assert "wash" not in pd.read_csv("venue_b_tape.csv").columns

The habit to keep. Write the declaration before you run a detector. Store sizes as integers so the volume identity is an equality. Assert the twin property, and break it once on purpose to see it fail. Keep the label in the harness and out of every file a detector can read. And say "share of reported volume", never just "70 percent wash".

Check yourself

Three questions

A colleague plants a bot "at 70 percent" on venue B and is surprised the tape has 21,312 trades, of which only 3,000 are honest. What does the 70 percent mean?

w is a share of reported volume. Wash reaching 70 percent of the total means wash is 0.7 / 0.3 times honest volume. The share of trades is a different and larger number here, because the round-lot bot's sizes are not the honest traders' sizes; quoting one as the other misdescribes the tape.

The washer sells 50 units at 00:01:36 and buys 50 units at 00:01:37. What did that pair do?

Volume sums the size of every print, so both legs count. Position is buys minus sells, and the pair is one of each at the same size, so it nets to zero. Double in volume, zero in position: that is what makes it wash, and why the twin, not the single print, is the planted structure.

Why measure a wash detector on a synthetic tape with a declared structure rather than on real venues?

A detection rate and a false-positive rate both need a known label in the denominator. On a real tape nobody outside the washer has one. A planted bench supplies it, at the price of testing only the bots you planted, which is why the copy bot is planted too.

Before session 7

Homework

About forty minutes, with the build-along code.

Write a declaration for a bench of your own

Five honest seeds, three styles, five shares, the pair rule and the gap, on one page. Then generate the tapes and run steps 4 and 5 on every one; the count of failing assertions should be zero.

Break it twice, on purpose

Make the second leg the same side as the first and watch step 5 fail. Then make the loop stop one pair early and watch the share assertion fail. Bring both error messages to session 7, where the label file finally gets opened, by the harness and nobody else.
