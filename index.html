Learn Crypto with Phoebe

Crypto markets as a data problem. Not a technology tour and not a money course: the subject is the off-chain data a desk actually consumes, the exchange tape, the order book, the candles built from it, the venues and the indices that try to reconcile them. Harrowgate is a constructed data team that has to publish a daily reference level and a venue volume table, and every instinct it brings from a conventional market feed meets something missing. There is no close price, because nothing ever closes. There is no consolidated tape. Every venue reports its own volume with no auditor and a reason to inflate it. The unit everything is quoted in is itself a traded thing that can move. Six leader sessions on what that does to a decision, ten practitioner sessions on reading, building and testing the data, and a bench that runs the forensic detectors in the page on a declared synthetic tape. No coins, no prices, no venues named, and nothing here is investment advice.

If you come from a data team

The transfer map in one glance. A conventional analyst, engineer or scientist is not starting from zero here; some habits are worth more, some are missing, and some are dangerous. Leader session 6 has the long form and the 90-day ladder.

Carries over

  • Grain discipline: one row is one print, a candle is an aggregate, and knowing which you are looking at.
  • Reconciliation habits: two sources that disagree are a finding, not a bug to suppress.
  • Calibration before judgement: a threshold means nothing until you know how often it fires on clean data.
  • Stream thinking: late prints, watermarks, idempotent loads, all the same mechanics as any event feed.

Must learn

  • Tape anatomy: trades, book snapshots, depth, spread, and why a close is a convention you choose.
  • Index rulebooks: partitions, volume-weighted medians, staleness and outlier exclusion, venue screening.
  • Forensic tests for self-reported numbers: the first-digit law, roundness, clustering, calibrated on honest venues.
  • Unit-of-account restatement: every quoted price is a ratio, and the denominator can move.

Must unlearn

  • Trusting the source's own volume number because it came from the source.
  • Treating "the price" as a fact rather than one venue's last print at one moment.
  • Ranking or weighting by reported volume before asking who reported it and why.
  • Reading a daily return without asking which boundary made it a day.
